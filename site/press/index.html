In the Papers

News & Magazine Articles

Coverage of Steve’s work over the years, from the parrots of Brownsville to the wild river he knew so well.

Brownsville Journal; Colorful Flock Seeks Better Life in a Border City

The New York Times · June 5, 2000 · By Ross E. Milloy

A feature on Brownsville’s wild red-crowned parrots, with Steve as the expert guide. He traces the flock’s arrival to a 1982 freeze and deforestation in Mexico, explains how the birds adapted to city life, and is credited with helping win their protection and their status as Brownsville’s official bird.

Border Lights Illuminate Two Faces of Rio Grande

Los Angeles Times · October 18, 1997 · By Jesse Katz

Steve is the central voice in this story about the tension between protecting the Rio Grande’s rare wildlife (the ocelot and jaguarundi) and floodlighting the river for border enforcement. He captures the paradox in a line the reporter never forgot: you live with the contradiction. A vivid portrait of Steve the naturalist-hunter.

‘Floaters’: Death in the Rio Grande

Los Angeles Times · March 27, 1996 · By Jesse Katz

A serious investigative report on migrant drownings in the Rio Grande. Steve appears as a river naturalist, describing with unflinching candor how the river reclaims those who don’t make it across, and where, in his words, “a human being loses his dignity.”

A Naturalist and a Southerner

The Rio Grande Review · November–December 1992 · By Bill Young

A feature interview with “the Vámonos man,” published while the show was still running Saturdays at 6:30 on KRGV. Steve declines to be filed under any label, saying he is not necessarily an ecologist and refusing the “bleeding heart” category when it comes to wildlife and game fish. By then he had made more than eighty half-hour shows, ranging from a hundred miles out into the Gulf to the cliffs along the Rio Grande in Starr County. He talks about the wood-rat hunt at Ejido Pancho Villa as “a good example of a hunter-gatherer society that still exists just a short distance from the border,” and about videotaping Coahuiltecan cliff drawings in Tamaulipas, geometric figures grouped in thirteens. Asked about Mexico, he is unequivocal: everything he had accomplished, the show included, came from what he had done there, and he owed the country a debt that probably could never be repaid.

Letter to the Editor, in his defense

The Brownsville Herald · c. 1995 · By David N. Scott

A reader wrote in after the cancellation to argue that Steve had lost his show simply for stating a fact: that a protected red-crowned parrot nesting site had been destroyed, when the red-crowned parrot is Brownsville’s official bird and its nesting sites are protected by city ordinance. The writer praised his record of educating the Valley about its own wildlife, contrasted the distributor’s concern for paying customers with its indifference to the birds, and suggested, half in jest, that the parrots themselves ought to boycott the company.
